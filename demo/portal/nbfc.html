For NBFC risk & compliance साक्ष्य

Check your LSP's log. Don't just receive it.

RBI's digital lending rules leave you responsible for what your lending service providers do with borrower data. Today that oversight is a questionnaire and an annual audit. VICT gives you a running process that checks the LSP's egress log every few seconds, and tells you the moment its history changes.

Watch the alarm fire → Run a witness

What you receive

GET /egress/witness/checkpoint
{"lsp_id": "lsp-acme",
 "seq":    1842,
 "head":   "9bc14e…",   # 32-byte hash
 "ts":     "2026-10-03T09:14:02Z",
 "sig":    "Ed25519…"}  # key you pinned

Plus the 32-byte digest of each new entry. No names, no PAN, no scores, no bank statements. Illustrative values.

On every poll

What raises an alarm.

Alarms are sticky. Once raised, they survive restarts until someone on your side investigates and clears them.

AlarmWhat it meansLikely cause
rollbackEntries you already witnessed have disappeared.The LSP deleted or truncated its log.
does-not-extend-witnessed-headThe new head doesn't build on the one you hold.An entry was edited and the chain rebuilt.
forkTwo different heads signed for the same sequence number.The LSP showed different histories to different parties.
bad-signatureThe checkpoint wasn't signed by the key you pinned.Key swapped, or someone else is answering.
wrong-lspThe checkpoint names a different LSP.Misconfiguration, or a substituted log.
digest-count-mismatchThe LSP sent the wrong number of digests.Entries withheld from the witness.
time-went-backwardsThe checkpoint is older than one already accepted.A replayed checkpoint.
lsp-unreachable signalThe LSP didn't answer this poll.Downtime. Reported, but not treated as tampering.

Read this before you rely on it

What it proves, and what it doesn't.

It proves

  • History you've witnessed hasn't been edited, deleted or forked since.
  • The LSP signed each checkpoint, so it can't later deny publishing it.
  • The LSP can only rewrite entries newer than your last poll: at a 10-second interval, that's at most 10 seconds of history.
  • You did it without receiving a single field of borrower data.

It doesn't prove

  • That every call was logged. The LSP runs the proxy. Network lockdown makes a call that skips the proxy fail, and verify_lockdown.sh lets you check that on their infrastructure.
  • Where vendors store data. The ledger shows which hosts received data, not their data centres.
  • That vendors erased data after a withdrawal. It tells you which ones to ask.

Run it on your side

One process. Your schedule.

The witness pulls; the LSP doesn't push. So the LSP can't choose when to report, and a quiet LSP shows up as a signal instead of silence.

  1. Get the LSP's checkpoint public key, and pin it.
  2. Run the witness against their gateway URL.
  3. Route its alarms to whoever owns LSP oversight.
At the NBFC
python -m src.egress.witness \
    --lsp-url https://vict.lsp.example.in \
    --lsp-id lsp-acme \
    --public-key lsp-checkpoint.pub.pem \
    --state witness-state.json \
    --interval 10
